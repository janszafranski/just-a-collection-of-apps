Chapter 1

Getting Started

PAGEdesigner is a page-layout program in the tradition of Serif’s PagePlus. You place text, pictures, shapes and tables on a page exactly where you want them, and print the result or export a PDF. This chapter introduces the workspace and the everyday jobs of opening, saving and viewing a publication.

Starting the program

Launch PAGEdesigner from the CREATORsuite launcher or by running pagecreator. It opens on the Startup Assistant, where you choose how to begin — from a ready-made design template, from a blank paper size, or by re-opening recent work.

Starting from a design template

The quickest way to a finished document is to start from a design. The Assistant opens on the Design Templates gallery — posters, flyers, a newsletter, a café menu, business cards, a certificate, an invitation and social-media posts, grouped by kind.

Click a template and it opens as a document already laid out. Replace the placeholder headings and text with your own words, double-click a picture frame to drop in a photo, then print or export a PDF. Everything is editable — move, recolour or delete anything you like.

Creating a new publication

Prefer a blank page? Choose New Publication and pick a paper size.

Paper sizes

In the Assistant’s New Publication list, pick a paper size. The presets are grouped:

  • Documents — A4, A4 Landscape, A5, A3, US Letter, US Legal;
  • Business — business cards, letterhead, DL flyers, postcards, compliment slips;
  • Booklet & Folded — A5/A4 booklets, greeting cards, tri-folds;
  • Large Format — A3, A2 and A1 posters.

Each thumbnail is drawn to the sheet’s true proportions. Prefer your own dimensions? Enter a width, height and page count under Custom size and click Create.

Opening and saving

Use File ▸ Open for an existing .pagc publication, or the Open page of the Startup Assistant for recent work. File ▸ Save (Ctrl+S) stores your document; Save As makes a copy. By default documents are saved to your shared Documents/just a… folder, so the CREATORsuite launcher can find them again.

PAGEdesigner also opens files from the program it replaces: File ▸ Open PagePlus… reads a Serif PagePlus .ppp publication, and File ▸ Open WebPlus Site… imports a WebPlus site, each as an editable document (your original is never changed).

The workspace

A new document opens with the page in the centre and everything you need around it.

Down the left is the tool palette; along the top, the menu bar and toolbar; to the right, the tabbed panels (Pages, Swatches, Line, Transparency, Transform, Layers and the Colour picker). The rulers frame the page, and the status bar along the bottom shows the current page and zoom.

Moving around and zooming

Change the zoom with the control at the bottom-right, with Ctrl+scroll, or with the Zoom tool. View ▸ Fit Page and Fit Width frame the page quickly. The Pan tool (or holding the space bar) slides the page around when you are zoomed in.

Chapter 2

Templates and Assets

You don’t have to start from a blank page. PAGEdesigner comes with ready-made design templates to build on, and a library of assets — callouts, badges, banners and decorations — to drop onto any page. Both save you time and both stay completely editable.

Starting from a template

When the program opens, the Startup Assistant shows the Design Templates gallery: finished layouts grouped by kind — posters, flyers, a newsletter, a café menu, business cards, a certificate, an invitation, vouchers and social-media posts. Click one and it opens as a document already laid out.

A template is a normal document: there is nothing special about it once it opens. Replace the placeholder headings and body text with your own words, double-click a picture frame to drop in a photo, recolour anything you like, and delete what you don’t need. When it’s ready, print it or export a PDF exactly as you would any other document.

The Assets library

Choose Insert ▸ Asset… for a gallery of ready-made graphics to add to the page you’re working on — the finishing touches that make a poster or a flyer pop.

Assets come in five groups:

  • Callouts — speech and thought bubbles, and a tag for pointing something out;
  • Badges — a SALE starburst, a “50% OFF” flash, a “NEW” roundel, a quality seal and a star;
  • Banners — a ribbon, a pennant flag and a title bar for section headings;
  • Arrows & pointers — arrows, a chevron and a lightning bolt;
  • Decorative — a sunburst, a star, a heart and page dividers.

Assets stay editable

Every asset is built from the same shapes and text the rest of the program uses, so once it’s on the page it behaves like anything else: drag it where you want it, resize it, change its colours from the Colour panel, and — on a badge or banner — just click the words and retype them. A “SALE” badge becomes a “½ PRICE” badge in seconds; a green ribbon becomes a red one with one colour change.

Making your own

Anything you build from shapes, text and pictures can be reused: lay it out once, then copy and paste it onto another page or into another document. Combined with templates and the asset library, that means most everyday jobs start from something, not from nothing.

Chapter 3

Setting Up Pages

Every publication is a stack of pages that share a paper size and, usually, a common background. This chapter covers adding and arranging pages, master pages for repeating content, and the guides and rulers that keep a layout tidy.

Pages and the Pages tab

The Pages tab lists every page in the document. Use the buttons beneath it to add a page, delete the current one, or move a page up or down the running order. Double-click a page to go to it.

Master pages

Anything that should appear on every page — a logo, a footer, a page number, a watermark — belongs on a master page. A master sits behind the ordinary pages that use it, so you draw the object once and it shows through everywhere.

Create one with New master page, edit it with Edit master page, and set which master a page uses from Master properties. Built a page already and realised several items repeat? Arrange ▸ Move Repeated Objects to Master Page… lifts them onto the master for you.

Rulers, guides and the grid

Drag out from a ruler to drop a guide line; objects snap to guides as you move them, which makes columns and margins easy to keep even. The ruler’s corner toggles between measurement origins. Turn snapping and the layout grid on or off from the View menu.

Headers, footers and page numbers

Put headers and footers on a master page so they repeat. For automatic page numbers, add a text frame to the master and insert a page-number field — each page then shows its own number without any manual updating.

Chapter 4

Working with Objects

Text frames, pictures, shapes and tables are all objects. Whatever the kind, you select, move, size, arrange and align them in the same way — the skills in this chapter apply everywhere in the program.

Selecting and moving

With the Pointer tool, click an object to select it; Shift-click to add more to the selection, or drag a band across several at once. Drag any selected object to move it; the handles around it resize it, and the round handle above it rotates it.

The Transform panel

For exact control, open the Transform tab and type an object’s position, width, height or rotation angle. This is the reliable way to make two objects the same size, or to place something a precise distance from the page edge.

Aligning and distributing

Arrange ▸ Align lines selected objects up — left edges, centres, right edges, tops or bottoms — either to each other or to the page. Distribute spaces three or more objects evenly. Alignment is what turns a rough arrangement into a crisp one.

Order, grouping and locking

Objects stack in the order you create them. Arrange sends a selection to the front or back, or forward and back one step at a time, so a caption can sit over a picture. Group ties several objects together so they move as one; Lock / Unlock pins something in place so you can’t nudge it by accident.

Chapter 5

Text

Text is the heart of most publications. PAGEdesigner handles it the PagePlus way — in frames you can link and flow, with artistic text for headlines, and full control over fonts, paragraphs and styles.

Text frames

Choose the Text tool and drag a rectangle to make a text frame. Type into it and the text wraps inside the frame’s edges. A frame is the basic container for body copy.

Flowing and linking frames

When a frame holds more text than it can show, an overflow marker appears. Drag a link from that marker to another frame and the text flows on — exactly how a newspaper runs a story across columns and pages. Resize or delete a frame and the text re-flows through the chain automatically.

Artistic text

Where frame text is for paragraphs, artistic text is a single line you can scale to any size — the tool for mastheads, titles and pull-quotes. It stays crisp at any size because it is vector, not a picture of type.

Fonts and paragraphs

Select some text and use the Format menu and the text panel to set the font, size, weight (bold), italic and underline, the text colour, the alignment (left, centred, right or justified) and the line spacing. Changes apply to whatever you have selected — a word, a line or a whole frame.

A drop cap (Format menu) enlarges the first letter of a paragraph and sets it into the lines below, the classic way to open an article or a story.

Text styles

When a document has several headings, you want them all to look the same. Text styles make that automatic: instead of setting the font, size and colour of each heading by hand, you apply a named style — a Heading, a Subheading, Body — from the Format menu, and every piece of text with that style matches. They keep a long document consistent and make a global restyle a one-step job.

Bulleted and numbered lists

To turn a run of short points into a list, select the paragraphs and choose a bullet or number style from Format ▸ Lists — a disc, a circle or a square for bullets; 1-2-3, a-b-c or i-ii-iii for numbers. The list indents and lines up neatly, and numbered lists renumber themselves if you add or remove an item.

Fitting text

When a headline is a shade too big or too small for its frame, resize the frame and the artistic text scales with it; for frame text, adjust the size until it sits comfortably, or link the frame to another so a long story flows on rather than overrunning. The red overflow marker in the corner of a frame tells you there is more text than the frame can show.

Wrapping text, spell check and tables from text

Text can wrap around a picture or shape so the words flow neatly around the artwork rather than sitting behind it. Before you print, run Format ▸ Check Spelling… (F7) to catch typos. And if you’ve typed a block of tab- or comma-separated data, convert text to a table turns it into a real, editable table in one step.

Chapter 6

Tables, Charts, Calendars and More

Beyond free text, PAGEdesigner builds the structured pieces a real publication needs: tables for data, charts that picture it, QR codes, a calendar wizard, and a mail merge for personalised runs.

Tables

Insert ▸ Table drops a grid you type straight into — a price list, a timetable, the “Dates for the diary” box in the sample newsletter. Choose the number of rows and columns, give the header row its own fill, and drag the borders to size the whole table or individual columns.

Charts

Insert ▸ Chart… turns a short table of figures into a bar, line or pie chart. Type one “label, value” per line, pick the type and give it a title; the chart draws itself and prints crisply in the PDF. Double-click a chart any time to change its data or type.

QR codes

Insert ▸ QR Code… builds a scannable QR code from a web address, an email (mailto:…), a phone number (tel:…) or any text. Resize it like a picture and it stays sharp — print it on a flyer or poster so a phone camera jumps straight to your page.

The Calendar wizard

Insert ▸ Calendar… generates a monthly or yearly calendar you can drop onto a page and style — handy for a community newsletter or a printed planner.

Mail Merge

Mail Merge… takes a list of names and addresses from a CSV file and produces one copy of the document per row, filling placeholder fields with each person’s details — for letters, labels or personalised flyers.

Chapter 7

Pictures

Pictures bring a page to life. PAGEdesigner keeps them in frames you can crop and style, and hands off to PAINTcanvas when a photo needs real editing.

Picture frames

Choose the Picture tool and drag a box to create a picture frame. A new frame shows a placeholder until you give it an image; double-click it to choose a photo. Keeping pictures in frames means you can design the layout first and drop the photographs in later.

Fitting, cropping and borders

A picture fits its frame — filling it or sitting inside it — and you can crop to show just the part you want. Round the frame’s corners or give it an outline from the Line panel to finish it off.

Editing a photo

Need to brighten, retouch or cut out a photo? Edit in PAINTcanvas sends the picture across to the image editor; when you save there, the edited version comes straight back into your page.

Chapter 8

Shapes, Lines and QuickShapes

Shapes and lines structure a page and add visual interest — a coloured band for a masthead, a rule between stories, a star on a special offer. PAGEdesigner’s QuickShapes give you twenty ready outlines, each with a fill, an outline and optional effects.

Drawing shapes and lines

The shape tools draw rectangles (with optional rounded corners), ellipses and straight lines. Hold a modifier while dragging to constrain a square or circle. A thin filled rectangle makes a neat rule.

QuickShapes

QuickShapes are ready-made outlines — triangles, diamonds, polygons, stars, arrows, a chevron, a cross, a heart, a lightning bolt, a speech bubble and a cloud. Pick one and drag it out, then resize and recolour it like any other shape.

Fills, outlines, dashes and arrowheads

Every shape has a fill and an outline; lines can be dashed and finished with arrowheads. Set them in the Colour and Line panels — a dashed line with an arrowhead makes a simple callout.

Drop shadow and glow

Filter effects lift an object off the page. A drop shadow adds depth; an outer glow draws the eye to a highlight or a price. Both are adjustable — colour, blur, offset and strength.

Chapter 9

Colour, Fills and Transparency

Colour ties a publication together. PAGEdesigner offers solid colours, swatches you keep to hand, gradient fills and adjustable transparency.

Applying colour

The Colour picker sets a solid fill or outline for the selected object — choose a colour with the sliders or type a hex value. The Fill, Line and Text tabs at the top of the picker say which part of the object you are colouring.

Swatches

The Swatches tab keeps a palette of colours one click away, so a document stays consistent. Save a colour you use often and reuse it across every object rather than matching hex values by hand.

Colour schemes

A colour scheme is a small set of coordinated colours applied across the whole publication at once. Picking a different scheme recolours every object that uses it, so you can try a document in several looks — a warm scheme, a cool one, a mono one — in seconds, without touching each item.

Gradient fills

Beyond flat colour, a gradient fades one colour smoothly into another across a shape — a sky behind a headline, a soft panel behind text. Set the two colours and the direction from the fill controls; the gradient scales with the object.

Transparency

Transparency lets what is behind an object show through it. A half-transparent coloured rectangle over a photo makes a tint you can read white text on; a faint shape becomes a watermark. Adjust an object’s transparency from the Transparency tab.

Chapter 10

Checking and Publishing

When the layout is ready, PAGEdesigner helps you check it and then put it out into the world — on paper or as a PDF.

Preflight and spell check

Before publishing, run a final Check Spelling… (F7) and look over the page for anything overlapping or running off the edge. Catching problems here saves a reprint later.

Printing

File ▸ Print sends the publication to your printer. Choose the printer, the pages and the number of copies, and print.

Exporting a PDF

Export PDF… (Ctrl+E) writes a vector PDF — the standard way to send a document to a print shop or to share online. Because the output is vector, text and shapes stay razor-sharp at any size, and you can include bleed and crop marks for professional printing.

Exporting an eBook

Export eBook… writes a reflowable EPUB from the text on your pages — each page becomes a chapter, with a contents list, ready to read on a phone, tablet or e-reader. Where the PDF preserves your exact layout, the eBook lets the reader’s device choose the type size and flow the words to fit its screen.
