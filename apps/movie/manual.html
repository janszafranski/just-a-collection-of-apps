Chapter 1

Welcome to MOVIEeditor

just a… MOVIEeditor turns your clips, photos and music into a finished film. You lay everything out on a timeline, trim it, add titles and transitions, and export a single MP4 you can upload or share.

It is a ground-up recreation in the spirit of Serif’s MoviePlus — the same ideas (a media bin, a multi-track timeline, transitions, overlays and a render to disc), rebuilt for Linux. The picture is assembled and rendered by ffmpeg, so what you preview is what you export.

The window at a glance

Media bin (left)
Every clip, photo and sound you have imported. Double-click one to drop it on the timeline.
Preview (centre)
Shows the frame under the playhead. Build & Play preview renders the movie and plays it back.
Timeline (bottom)
Your tracks, stacked: overlay (V2) on top, main video (V1) in the middle, audio (A1) at the bottom. The red line is the playhead.
NoteThroughout, a numbered “To…” box is a procedure you can follow exactly; a Tip suggests a faster way; a Remember flags something easy to forget.
Needs ffmpegMOVIEeditor renders with ffmpeg. If exporting reports it is missing, install it — sudo pacman -S ffmpeg (Arch/CachyOS), sudo apt install ffmpeg (Mint/Ubuntu) or sudo dnf install ffmpeg (Fedora) — then reopen the app.
Chapter 2

Getting started

Starting a new movie

When you open MOVIEeditor the Startup wizard appears. Pick the size and frame rate your finished film should be — most web and phone video is 1280×720 at 30 frames per second — or open a project you saved before.

To start a new movie

  1. Choose a Project size (for example 1280×720 (HD)) and a Frame rate.
  2. Click Start new movie. An empty timeline opens at that size.
  3. Or click Open a project… to carry on with a saved .movp file.
TipYou can change the size and frame rate at any time from Timeline ▸ Project settings…. The final resolution is chosen again when you export.

Importing your clips

Nothing can go on the timeline until it is in the Media bin. Importing only references your files — it never moves or alters the originals.

To import media

  1. Click Import media… at the top of the Media bin, or press Ctrl+I.
  2. Select one or more video clips, photos or sound files and click Open.
  3. Each appears in the bin with a thumbnail. Hover for its name and length.

MOVIEeditor reads the common formats: video (MP4, MOV, AVI, MKV, WebM, WMV…), photos (PNG, JPEG, GIF, WebP…) and sound (MP3, WAV, AAC, M4A, OGG, FLAC).

Putting a clip on the timeline

To add a clip

  1. Double-click an item in the Media bin — or select several and click + Add to timeline.
  2. Video and photos land on the V1 video track, one after another; sound lands on A1.
  3. Drag a clip left or right to move it; it snaps to its neighbours and the playhead.

Previewing

Click anywhere on the timeline ruler to move the playhead; the preview shows that frame. To watch the movie play, click Build & Play preview — MOVIEeditor renders a quick draft and plays it. Click ■ to stop and return to editing.

Chapter 3

The timeline

The timeline is where the film is assembled. Tracks stack from the overlay V2 at the top, down through the main video V1, to audio A1 at the bottom. Higher video tracks appear in front of lower ones.

Tracks

Add more tracks from Timeline ▸ Add video/audio track — another video track for extra overlays, or another audio track to layer music under narration. Video tracks are added at the top, audio tracks at the bottom.

Trimming & moving

To trim a clip

  1. Hover over a clip’s left or right edge — the pointer becomes a double arrow.
  2. Drag the edge inward to hide part of the clip, or outward to reveal more (up to the length of the original).
  3. Trimming the left edge of a video also moves where it starts playing from.
TipEdges and clips snap to nearby clip starts/ends and to the playhead, so cuts line up cleanly. Zoom in with the 🔍+ button for finer control.

Splitting

To split a clip in two

  1. Click the ruler to put the playhead where you want the cut.
  2. Select the clip, then press Ctrl+K (or Split at playhead).
  3. The clip becomes two independent clips you can trim, move or delete separately.

To remove a clip, select it and press Delete, or right-click it and choose Delete clip.

Markers

Markers are reminders pinned to moments in the timeline — a beat in the music, a line of dialogue. Put the playhead where you want one and press M (Timeline ▸ Add marker at playhead). They show as blue flags on the ruler. Clear markers removes them.

Chapter 4

Transitions

A transition is how one shot becomes the next — a gentle dissolve, a fade through black, a wipe. Without one, clips simply cut.

To add a transition between two shots

  1. Make sure the two clips touch on the same track (no gap between them).
  2. Right-click the second clip and open Transition (into clip).
  3. Choose a style — Dissolve, Fade through black, a Wipe or Slide, a Clock wipe, and more.
  4. A gold wedge on the clip’s left edge shows a transition is set.

Set a transition’s length in the clip’s Properties dialog. Separately, Fade in and Fade out (also in Properties) ramp a single clip up from, or down to, black — shown as a diagonal line across the clip’s corner.

RememberA transition blends a clip into the one before it, so it needs a clip on either side with no gap between them.
Chapter 5

Titles, captions & colour clips

Titles and captions

Titles are text clips. Put them on the overlay track (V2) and they appear over the video beneath; put one on its own and it shows against black — a classic title card.

To add a title

  1. Choose Timeline ▸ Add title… (or + Title under the Media bin).
  2. Type your text, then set the size, colour, and where it sits on screen (horizontal and vertical).
  3. Give it weight with Bold/Italic, an Outline (pixels of black edge for legibility over any footage), or tick Draw a box behind the text for a caption bar.
  4. Pick an Animation — Fade in & out, Slide in from left/right, or Rise up — so the title moves on and off rather than just appearing.
  5. Drag the title on the timeline to when it should appear; trim it to set how long it stays.

Colour clips

A colour clip is a solid, full-frame colour — handy as a title background, a quick intro card, or a flash of colour between scenes.

To add a colour clip

  1. Choose Timeline ▸ Add colour clip… and pick a colour.
  2. It is added to the video track; trim it to the length you want and drop a title over it.
Chapter 6

Motion: pan & zoom, speed, rotate

Pan & zoom (Ken Burns)

A still photo need not sit still. Pan & zoom slowly pushes into the picture over its time on screen, giving stills the life of moving footage — the “Ken Burns” look.

To add pan & zoom to a photo

  1. Right-click the photo on the timeline.
  2. Tick Ken Burns (pan & zoom). A ⤢ badge appears on the clip.

Slow motion & fast forward

Change a video clip’s Speed in its Properties dialog. Below 1× is slow motion (0.5× is half speed); above is fast forward. The clip’s sound is stretched or compressed to match, so it stays in sync.

Rotate

Rotate (also in Properties) turns a clip by any angle — straighten a wonky horizon, or tilt a shot for effect.

Crop

Tick Crop this clip in Properties to keep just part of the frame — cut away letterbox bars or push in on the action. Set the crop’s left/top corner and its width/height as fractions of the frame.

Shrink & place (2D transform)

Use Shrink & place to scale a clip below full size and position it on a black frame — a framed photo, a split-screen tile, or the base for a title card.

Chapter 7

Effects & colour

Every video, photo and colour clip can carry a look: a one-click effect and fine colour adjustments, set in the clip’s Properties dialog.

Effects

Pick one from the Effect list: Black & white, Sepia, Vignette, Soft focus, Sharpen, Old film, Warm, Cool, Negative or Mirror.

Colour adjustments

Nudge Brightness, Contrast and Saturation to balance a shot — rescue something shot too dark, or make colours sing. Effects and adjustments stack with each other and with motion.

Chapter 8

Overlays: picture-in-picture & chroma key

Clips on a higher video track (V2) sit in front of the main picture. Shrink one into a corner for picture-in-picture, or key out its background with chroma key.

Picture-in-picture

To make a picture-in-picture

  1. Put the clip on the V2 overlay track, over the main footage.
  2. Open its Properties and tick Picture-in-picture overlay.
  3. Set its size (a fraction of the frame) and its x/y position (0–1).

Chroma key (green screen)

Shot someone against a green or blue sheet? Chroma key makes that colour transparent so the track beneath shows through.

To key out a background

  1. Put the clip on an overlay track, over the background you want to show through.
  2. In Properties, tick Chroma key and set the Key colour to the backdrop (bright green by default).

Overlays also have an Opacity setting — below 1.0 they become see-through, for ghosting and double-exposure looks — and honour Fade in/Fade out, which dissolve the overlay on and off rather than cutting.

Moving a picture-in-picture

Tick Animate PiP across the screen and set an end x/y: the inset glides from its start position to the end over the clip — a lower-third that slides in, or a video that drifts across the frame.

Blend modes

A Blend mode changes how an overlay’s colours mix with the picture beneath, instead of simply covering it — Screen and Lighten for light leaks and glows, Multiply and Darken for textures and shadows, and more.

TipPicture-in-picture, movement, chroma key, blend modes, effects, adjustment, rotate, crop and opacity can all be combined on one overlay clip.
Chapter 9

Audio

Sound sits on audio tracks (A1, A2…). A video clip keeps its own sound too — both are mixed into the final film.

To add music or narration

  1. Import the sound file (Ctrl+I) and double-click it — it lands on the audio track.
  2. Drag it to line up with the picture; trim its ends to fit.

Each clip’s Properties give you:

  • Volume — a multiplier; 0 is silent, 1 is as recorded, higher is louder.
  • Pan — place the sound left (−1) to right (+1).
  • Fade in / Fade out — ramp the level up at the start and down at the end, so music never starts or stops abruptly.
TipTo duck background music under a voice, lower the music clip’s Volume (say to 0.3) for the stretch where someone is speaking — split the music clip (Ctrl+K) around that stretch first.

Waveforms

Each audio clip on the timeline shows its waveform — the shape of the sound. Use it to line cuts up with a beat or a spoken word by eye.

Recording narration

Add a voice-over straight from your microphone.

To record narration

  1. Move the playhead to where the voice-over should begin.
  2. Choose Timeline ▸ Record narration… and speak — a timer shows the length.
  3. Click Stop & add to timeline. The recording is saved and dropped on the audio track at the playhead, ready to trim and mix.
Needs a microphoneNarration records through PulseAudio/PipeWire. If nothing is captured, check that a microphone is connected and selected as your system input.
Chapter 10

Exporting your movie

When the film is ready, export it to a single MP4 — the format every phone, browser and video site understands.

To export

  1. Choose File ▸ Export movie (MP4)… (Ctrl+E).
  2. Pick a Size — 1080p (Full HD), 720p (ideal for web and YouTube), 480p (small file) or Match project.
  3. Pick a Quality — higher is better but larger.
  4. Choose where to save and click Save. A progress bar shows each stage; the finished file lands in your Pictures ▸ just a… folder by default.
NoteExport renders the full-quality film from scratch, so it takes longer than the draft preview — longer films and higher sizes take more time. The progress bar names each stage (clips, titles, audio, finishing).

Saving your project

Saving the project (Ctrl+S, a .movp file) is separate from exporting the movie. The project remembers your timeline, trims and settings so you can come back and keep editing; the exported MP4 is the finished film. Projects save to your Documents ▸ just a… folder and reopen from the CREATORsuite launcher.

Glossary

Playhead
The red line marking the current moment; the preview shows its frame.
Track
A lane on the timeline. Video tracks stack front-to-back; audio tracks mix together.
Transition
The blend from one clip to the next — dissolve, fade, wipe, slide.
Ken Burns
A slow zoom/pan across a still photo to give it motion.
Chroma key
Making one colour (a green/blue screen) transparent so another track shows through.
Picture-in-picture
A smaller video inset over the main picture.
Render
Building the actual video file from your timeline — what preview and export do.
.movp
A MOVIEeditor project file: your editable timeline, not the finished movie.
