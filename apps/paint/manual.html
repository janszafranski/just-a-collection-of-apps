Chapter 1

Getting Started

PAINTcanvas is a layer-based image editor in the tradition of Serif PhotoPlus. You open a photograph (or start a blank canvas), then paint, retouch and apply adjustments and filters. This chapter introduces the workspace and the basics of opening and saving.

Opening and creating images

File ▸ Open loads a picture; File ▸ New starts a blank canvas at a size and background colour you set. PAINTcanvas reads common formats including PNG and JPEG, and you can drag an image straight onto the canvas.

The workspace

The editor puts your picture in the centre, with the toolbox down the left, the tool’s own options along the top, and the Colour picker and Layers panel to the right.

Saving your work

File ▸ Save and Save As write a PNG (keeping any transparency) or a JPEG. Pictures save by default to your shared Pictures/just a… folder.

Chapter 2

The Canvas

Before editing the pixels, it helps to get the canvas itself right — its size, its framing and its orientation.

Image size and canvas size

Image ▸ Resize changes the picture’s pixel dimensions; Canvas Size adds or trims space around it without scaling the image — useful for adding a border.

Cropping and straightening

Crop keeps only the part you want; Straighten levels a crooked horizon. Both are on the Image menu.

Rotating and flipping

Rotate turns the whole image by 90° or any angle; Flip mirrors it horizontally or vertically.

Chapter 3

Painting and Drawing Tools

PAINTcanvas gives you the familiar toolbox for making marks — brushes for freehand work, shapes for geometry, and text for words.

Brush, pencil and eraser

The Brush paints soft strokes and the Pencil hard ones; the Eraser removes paint. Set size, hardness and opacity in the options bar.

Shapes and fill

Draw filled or outlined rectangles and ellipses, and pour colour into an area with the Fill (paint-bucket) tool. The Gradient tool blends one colour into another.

Text

The Text tool adds words to the picture in any installed font, size and colour — for a caption, a title or a watermark.

Chapter 4

Colour

Everything you paint uses the current colour. PAINTcanvas makes it easy to pick, sample and reuse colours.

The Colour picker

Choose a colour with the RGB sliders or by typing a hex value. The eyedropper lifts a colour from anywhere in the image so you can match it exactly.

Foreground and background

PAINTcanvas keeps a foreground and a background colour; tools use one or the other, and some — like the gradient — blend between them.

Chapter 5

Selections

A selection limits where your edits land, so you can change one part of a picture and leave the rest untouched.

Making a selection

The rectangular and elliptical selection tools drag out an area; everything you then paint, fill, adjust or filter affects only inside it. With nothing selected, edits apply to the whole layer.

Moving and clearing

Move a selection to reposition the marked area, and clear it (Esc) when you want to work on the whole image again.

Chapter 6

Layers

Layers are the heart of a non-destructive edit. Each layer is a transparent sheet you can paint on, reorder, fade or blend without harming the ones beneath.

The Layers panel

The Layers panel lists every layer, top to bottom. Add a new layer for fresh paint, hide a layer with its visibility toggle, and drag layers to reorder them.

Opacity and blend modes

Each layer has an opacity (how solid it is) and a blend mode (how its colours combine with the layers below — multiply, screen, overlay and more). Together they drive most montage and tinting effects.

Chapter 9

Retouching and Effects

The retouch tools fix the small flaws every real photograph has, and effects add finish.

Clone, red-eye, dodge and burn

The Clone tool paints over a blemish using another part of the picture; Red-eye fixes flash red; Dodge lightens and Burn darkens, exactly as in a darkroom.

Drop shadow and glow

On a layer with transparency, add a drop shadow or an outer glow to lift a cut-out subject off its background.

Chapter 10

Saving and Exporting

When the picture is finished, save it in the right format for the job.

PNG and JPEG

Save as PNG to keep sharp edges and transparency, or as JPEG for a small photographic file. Save As keeps your working copy while exporting another.

Hand off to DRAWmaster

Edit in DRAWmaster sends the picture to the vector drawing app; when you save there it comes back, so you can combine painted and drawn artwork.
